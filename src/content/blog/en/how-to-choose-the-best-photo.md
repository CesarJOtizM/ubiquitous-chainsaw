Thinking about commissioning a custom portrait of your pet, but not sure which photo to send? Don't worry. Choosing a good photograph is one of the first steps toward a portrait that captures your pet's essence.

You don't need a professional camera or a special photo shoot. Your phone can take an excellent picture, as long as you keep a few details in mind. Those details help me represent their features, colors, and character as faithfully as possible.

Here is what to look for before you send me your photos.

## 1. Distance matters

Try not to leave your pet too far away in the frame. When they look very small, it is hard to make out important details of the face, the eyes, and the fur.

The ideal distance is close enough to read their features clearly and, if possible, to see a good part of the body.

**My tip:** step a little closer and make sure your pet's details are easy to distinguish.

## 2. Please, no blur

A blurry photo makes painting much harder, because the eyes, fur texture, and small details of the face are not clear.

Look for a sharp image, one where your pet's features look defined.

That lets me study their character and work toward a portrait that looks as much like them as possible.

## 3. Light changes everything

Light is one of the most important things when you choose a photo.

When the image is too dark, some colors and features disappear into the shadows. Good light, on the other hand, shows the tones of the fur, the eyes, and the rest of their appearance.

**My tip:** look for a place with soft natural light. You can take the photo near a window, and avoid light that is too harsh or shadows that hide the face.

## 4. What does an ideal photo look like?

A good reference photo does not have to be professional. What matters is that it gives me the information I need to paint your pet.

These are the qualities I look for:

- **Good light:** so the real colors are visible.
- **Eyes in view:** so the gaze and its character can be read.
- **Defined fur:** so the different tones and textures can be seen.
- **Clear features:** so the shape of the face and its particularities are easy to identify.

Remember that every pet is unique. Those small details are exactly what make the portrait special.

## 5. Three quick tips for taking the photos

If you want to take new pictures of your pet, here are three simple recommendations:

1. **Find good light.** Take the photo near a window or in a well-lit place.
2. **Focus first.** Before you shoot, make sure the phone has focused and the image will not come out blurry.
3. **Step a little closer.** Choose a distance where the face reads well and the features are easy to see.

One more detail: skip filters that change your pet's colors. I need to see the real tones to get a result that stays as faithful as possible.

## 6. What if the portrait is a surprise gift?

We can do that too.

If you want to give a custom portrait and you cannot ask that person for a new photo without ruining the surprise, don't worry.

Gather several photos of their pet and send them to me. The more references I have, the better I can compare the features and help you choose the image that works best for the portrait.

You don't have to pick it on your own. I am happy to help.
